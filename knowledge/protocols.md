# Protocol Spec — one per state

Draft for curation. Each check-in state resolves to one protocol. Copy is written
in Dhyana's voice (see [BRAND.md](../BRAND.md)) so you can read what a user sees.

**How to curate:** for each, approve / edit the copy / cut it / swap the practice.
Mark changes inline or just tell me. Nothing here is built except where noted ✅.

**Evidence tags** (same honesty discipline as the knowledge base):
`strong` = multiple RCTs/meta-analysis · `moderate` = supported, real limits ·
`emerging` = suggestive · `traditional` = contemplative lineage, not a clinical claim.

**Shared anatomy** (every protocol): eyebrow → title → body → *(optional practice)* →
reflection *(free or structured)* → optional "why" line. Mirrors The Pause Lab's
intention → rationale → template shape.

## 1 · Depleted — *Restore* ✅ built (reflection proposed)

> Running on empty. Heavy. Hard to start.

- **Eyebrow:** Restore
- **Title:** Rest that actually lands
- **Body:** You're running on empty — and pushing harder won't refill it. Sit for a few minutes and let your attention settle below the busyness. No effort, nothing to fix. This is the kind of rest that reaches the nervous system, not just a pause between tasks.
- **Practice:** settle — 5 min timer ✅
- **Reflection — PROPOSED change to structured (1 field):**
  - *One thing you're setting down for tonight* — permission to stop carrying something
  - *(currently free-text: "Did you rest deeply today, or only stop working?")*
- **Why:** Depletion is a load the body carries over time, not just a mood — deep rest is what discharges it. Ordinary breaks often don't reach that far down.
- **Evidence:** deep rest / nervous-system restoration — `emerging` · `traditional`
- **Source:** Restoration capacity (capstone)
- **Design note:** alternative framing is *behavioral activation* ("one small thing you'll do") which has `strong` evidence for low/flat states — but it's activating, not restful. Pick the flavor: rest vs. gentle activation.

## 2 · Activated — *Name it* (proposed — the biggest Pause Lab port)

> Running hot. Stressed or over-driven.

- **Eyebrow:** Name it
- **Title:** Name what's running hot
- **Body:** You're activated. Before you do anything with it, name it. Putting a feeling into words — and rating how strong it is — is one of the fastest ways to take the edge off it.
- **Practice:** *(optional)* short settle, or skip — the naming is the practice
- **Reflection — STRUCTURED (3 fields):**
  - *Name the feeling* — tense · anxious · angry · overwhelmed · wired…
  - *How strong? (1–9)* — a rating control
  - *What set it off?* — the scene or trigger
- **Why:** Naming a feeling and rating it engages the thinking brain and measurably lowers its intensity. "Name it to tame it."
- **Evidence:** affect labeling — `strong` (Lieberman et al.)
- **Source:** The Pause Lab "name and rate your feelings" (notes 4 & 5)
- **Build note:** needs a new 1–9 rating control (small addition; not just text inputs).

## 3 · Dysregulated — *Reset* (proposed)

> Scattered. Mixed signals. Needs a reset.

- **Eyebrow:** Reset
- **Title:** Name the pulls
- **Body:** Mixed signals, pulled in two directions. This isn't the moment to decide anything big. Name what's pulling at you, pick one small next step, and let the rest wait.
- **Reflection — STRUCTURED (3 fields):**
  - *One thing pulling at me*
  - *Another thing pulling at me*
  - *One small next step*
- **Why:** Getting competing pulls out of your head lowers the load of holding them at once. A single concrete next step breaks the freeze.
- **Evidence:** cognitive offloading / CBT — `moderate`
- **Source:** The Pause Lab daily-review + CBT

## 4 · Regulated — *Sustain* ✅ BUILT

> Steady. Functioning well.

- **Eyebrow:** Sustain
- **Title:** You're steady
- **Body:** Nothing to fix — you're functioning well. So instead of fixing, bank it. Naming what's good, while you're already steady, builds the reserve you draw on when you're not.
- **Reflection — STRUCTURED (3 fields):** *Three good things from today* — each a line
- **Why:** *(none shown — kept clean for an okay state)*
- **Evidence:** three good things / savoring — `strong` (Seligman et al.)
- **Source:** The Pause Lab gratitude/accomplishment days (notes 6 & 7)

## 5 · Restful Alertness — *Savor* (proposed)

> Calm and clear. Fully present. Ready.

- **Eyebrow:** Savor
- **Title:** Restful alertness
- **Body:** Calm, clear, fully present. This is the state everything else is trying to return to — so don't rush past it. Savoring a good state helps it last, and makes it easier to find your way back.
- **Reflection — STRUCTURED (3 fields):**
  - *What got you here?*
  - *Where do you feel it?* — the body, in a word
  - *One word for this state*
- **Why:** *(none — clean)*
- **Evidence:** savoring — `moderate` (Bryant & Veroff)
- **Source:** The Pause Lab flow day (note 8) + positive psychology

## Cross-cutting decision for you

**Progressive depth.** The Pause Lab asks for real writing; Dhyana is "a line is
enough." Two options across all protocols:
- **A — Structured by default** (what Regulated does now): show the fields.
- **B — One line by default, "go deeper" reveals the structure.** Lower friction, respects the 30-second user; the journaler can expand.

Worth deciding once — it shapes how every protocol above feels. My lean: **B**,
for a Gen Z audience allergic to homework. But it's a real call, and yours.
