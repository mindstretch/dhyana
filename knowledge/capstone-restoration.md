# Capacity: Restoration

> The ability to regularly access deep rest and recover the nervous system —
> so that clarity, resilience, creativity, and meaning remain available under load.

**Source:** MVS 585 Capstone — *Restoring Human Potential: Chronic Stress,
Burnout, and the Need for a Deeper Model of Intervention* (T. Peterson, 2026).
**Vocabulary:** generalized per [generalization.md](generalization.md).
**Related capacities:** Resilience · Emotional Regulation · Attention · Expanded Awareness

---

## Concepts

### C1 · Allostatic load
**Definition.** The cumulative physiological burden of chronic stress — stress
written into the body across multiple biological systems, not just felt as a mood.
**Why it matters.** Reframes stress from "a bad day" to a measurable load that
accumulates. This is the hinge that makes *restoration* (not just coping) the
real intervention.
**Evidence:** `strong` — Guidi et al. (2021), systematic review; allostatic
load/overload associated with poorer health outcomes.
**Links:** underlies → C2 (Burnout); motivates → P1 (Rest must reach the nervous system).

### C2 · Burnout
**Definition.** A syndrome from chronic stress that was never successfully
managed — emotional exhaustion, cynicism/mental distance, reduced efficacy.
**Why it matters.** Locates the problem in the *person × environment*
interaction, not personal weakness. Removes shame; makes it addressable.
**Evidence:** `strong` — Passos et al. (2023); standard occupational definition (WHO/ICD-11).
**Links:** expression of → C1; obstructs → C4 (Direction of growth).

### C3 · Pure awareness
*(the settled ground of mind)*
**Definition.** The quiet basis of the mind beneath its activity — awareness
itself, distinct from the thoughts moving through it. A level you can settle into,
not a thing you look at.
**Why it matters.** Supplies a *different starting point*: the person is not
fundamentally their exhaustion. There is a steadier level beneath the activation
to return to.
**Evidence:** `traditional` — described across contemplative traditions;
experiential, not a clinical claim. `lineage:` MVS "Being / pure consciousness."
**Links:** accessed via → C6 (Restful alertness); basis for → P3 (Settle, then act).

### C4 · Direction of growth
**Definition.** The premise that human development naturally trends toward
greater wellbeing, capability, and fulfillment when obstacles are removed —
growth is the default, not the exception.
**Why it matters.** Reframes burnout as *obstructed development*, not just lost
productivity. Raises the standard of what recovery is for.
**Evidence:** `traditional`. `lineage:` MVS "expansion of happiness is the purpose of life."
**Links:** obstructed by → C2; the "why" behind the whole capacity.

### C5 · Stillness and action, integrated
**Definition.** A whole life holds both — inner quiet *and* engaged outer action —
without rejecting either. Rest is not retreat from work; it's the ground you work from.
**Why it matters.** The product's honest boundary: this is **not** withdrawal
from work, ambition, or family. Protects against the "just meditate more" trap.
**Evidence:** `traditional`. `lineage:` MVS "integration of absolute and relative values."
**Links:** balances → C3 with ordinary life; grounds → P3.

### C6 · Restful alertness
**Definition.** Deep physiological rest with the mind awake — distinct from sleep
(body rests, mind off) and ordinary waking (mind on, body active).
**Why it matters.** Names the specific state restoration is after. A strained
nervous system may not be restored by *more effort* — it may need activity to
settle at its source.
**Evidence:** `emerging` — a rest-with-wakefulness state is described across the
settling-meditation literature; the precise physiological signature is less
settled than the outcome data. `lineage:` MVS "restful alertness."
**Links:** the mechanism proposed for E1–E3; direct experience of → C3.

## Principles

### P1 · Rest must reach the nervous system, not just the schedule
Most people already *know* they should sleep, exercise, set boundaries. The gap
isn't information — it's that the nervous system is too strained for ordinary
rest to fully restore balance. Restoration targets the *instrument*, not the
to-do list.
`derived from` C1 + C2.

### P2 · Symptom management is necessary but incomplete
Wellness programs, therapy, workload changes — all can help, and the honest
evidence says so (Cohen et al. 2023). But they often address effects,
short-term, with weak follow-up. A durable model must also rebuild recovery
capacity itself.
`evidence:` `moderate` — Cohen et al. (2023), systematic review, notes design limitations.

### P3 · Settle, then act
The quality of action depends on the state it arises from. Move inward first
(the arrow drawn back on the bow), then act — inner settling as the basis of
*more* effective outer action, not less.
`traditional` · `lineage:` Maharishi on Bhagavad-Gita 2.48 (*yogasthah kuru
karmani*). This is the bridge from "rest" to "performance" that makes the
capacity matter to working professionals.

## Evidence (what the experiments rest on)

| ID | Finding | Population | Strength | Honest caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | ↓ perceived stress, depression, burnout | 40 therapeutic-school staff, RCT | `moderate` · studied via TM | single site, self-report, wait-list control |
| R2 | ↓ emotional exhaustion, insomnia, anxiety | 80 healthcare workers, RCT | `moderate` · studied via TM | **primary endpoint (acute distress) not significant** |
| R3 | ↓ emotional exhaustion; ↑ resilience, ↓ stress/fatigue | 78 teachers/staff, RCT | `moderate` · studied via TM | no biomarkers; 4-month follow-up |
| R4 | ↓ trait anxiety, largest effect at high baseline | meta-analysis of RCTs | `strong` · studied via TM | anxiety ≠ burnout, but travels with it |

> **Two honesty notes, carried from the source and the generalization rule:**
>
> 1. R2's headline number *failed* — and we say so. The secondary findings
>    (exhaustion, insomnia, anxiety) are what hold up, and those are the
>    chronic-stress measures that matter most.
> 2. Every row above studied **Transcendental Meditation specifically** — a
>    taught, mantra-based technique. Our shippable *silent settling practice* is
>    a generic approximation. These studies support the **direction**; they do
>    not guarantee an app practice replicates the effect. Generic settling has
>    its own broader evidence base (mindfulness/MBSR) that we still need to
>    bring in — that's research, not relabeling.

## Experiments (n=1 protocols)

Practice referenced generically as a **silent settling practice**: sit quietly,
let attention settle below active thought using a light anchor, no forcing.

### E1 · Twice-daily settling → emotional exhaustion
- **Hypothesis:** Twice-daily settling reduces my emotional exhaustion over 4 weeks.
- **Protocol:** Morning + evening sessions (start 10 min). Same times daily.
- **Measure:** 1-item exhaustion check-in each evening (0–10); weekly 3-item burnout mini-scale.
- **Duration:** 28 days. **Confound to log:** sleep, workload spikes.
- **Verdict rule:** trend + your own read, not a p-value. `personal` evidence only.
- `from` R1, R3 · `targets` C2.

### E2 · Inward-before-action → decision quality
- **Hypothesis:** A short settling before demanding work improves how clear/effective my actions feel.
- **Protocol:** On 3 high-load workdays/week, 5 min settling *before* the hardest block. 2 days as control.
- **Measure:** end-of-block rating — reactivity (0–10), clarity (0–10), one sentence on quality.
- **Duration:** 2 weeks. **Verdict rule:** compare settling vs. control days.
- `from` P3 · `targets` C6, P3.

### E3 · Restful alertness vs. sleep debt
- **Hypothesis:** On low-sleep days, a midday settling restores more usable energy than pushing through.
- **Protocol:** On days with <6h sleep, alternate: settling day vs. push-through day.
- **Measure:** 3pm energy (0–10), evening irritability (0–10).
- **Duration:** until ~6 of each. **Verdict rule:** paired comparison.
- `from` C6 · `targets` C6.

## Reflection prompts (experience → data)

Tagged so the AI layer can later cluster responses into themes.

- **[exhaustion]** Where in today did I feel most depleted — and what preceded it?
- **[recovery]** Did I rest *deeply* today, or only stop working? What's the difference for me?
- **[reactivity]** When did I react from strain today instead of choosing? What was the trigger?
- **[source]** Was my best action today driven by pressure or by steadiness?
- **[meaning]** Did work feel like expression or depletion today? One line on why.
